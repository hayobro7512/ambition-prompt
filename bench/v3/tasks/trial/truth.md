# Answer key (judge only)
1. Source 1 is vendor marketing with no numbers; it isn't evidence for this business.
2. Source 3 is far too small: 24 vs ~22 conversions; a 1.2-point difference at a 12% base needs roughly 11,700 trials per arm to detect (16·p(1-p)/d² ≈ 16·0.12·0.88/0.012²), which at 820 trials a month split between two arms takes about 29 months. Its "+10% relative" is noise-level.
3. Source 2 shows a deadline effect: 38% of conversions happen in days 12-14 after the reminder, so a 30-day trial likely delays those conversions by ~16 days (worse months-to-revenue) and may not raise the total.
4. Cost: a 30-day trial roughly doubles per-trial cost (~$9 per 14 days -> ~$19), small next to $1,140 first-year revenue, so cost is not the deciding factor; delay and lack of evidence are.
5. Source 5 is a different segment: enterprise pilots go through sales and can get 30-45 days without changing the self-serve trial.
6. Recommendation: keep 14 days for self-serve; give sales an extended pilot option; if the CEO wants evidence, test a variant (e.g. 21 or 30 days, or a "request an extension" button) with a pre-set sample size and a revenue-timing metric, knowing it needs many months at current volume.
Weak: recommending 30 days on the blog's word; treating the 21-day test as proof; ignoring the deadline effect; ignoring the enterprise segment difference; invented numbers.
